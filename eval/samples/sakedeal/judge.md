### Warm or cold?
Kimoto and yamahai are generally better suited to warming: their natural lactic fermentation gives more acidity and umami, which open up beautifully with heat. Sokujo's cleaner base is what most fragrant, fruity ginjo are built on, and those delicate aromas are best enjoyed chilled.
Gentle warmth softens the acidity of a kimoto or yamahai, rounds out the body and brings the savoury flavours forward. Many of these sakes show their best side at 40 to 50°C. In an aromatic ginjo or daiginjo, heat flattens the fruity notes of melon, pear and apple, so I serve those at 8 to 15°C.
That does not make every sokujo a cold sake. A full-bodied sokujo junmai or honjozo can be lovely warm. Namazake (unpasteurised sake) and sparkling sake are best kept cold, whatever the starter.
### The temperature ladder
Japan has a name for almost every serving temperature. Use this as a guide:
Name	Temperature	Works well for
Yuki-hie (snow cold)	5°C	Sparkling sake, namazake
Hana-hie (flower cold)	10°C	Ginjo, daiginjo
Suzu-hie (cool)	15°C	Ginjo, lighter junmai
Jo-on (room temperature)	about 20°C	Junmai, kimoto, yamahai
Hinata-kan (sun warm)	30°C	A gentle first step for kimoto and yamahai
Hitohada-kan (body warm)	35°C	Kimoto, yamahai, junmai
Nuru-kan (lukewarm)	40°C	Kimoto, yamahai, junmai
Jo-kan (warm)	45°C	Kimoto, yamahai
Atsu-kan (hot)	50°C	Yamahai, robust junmai and honjozo
Tobikiri-kan (extra hot)	55°C and above	Robust futsushu
### Warming sake at home
Pour the sake into a tokkuri (sake flask) or a small heat-proof carafe.
Place it in a pan of hot water that is just off the boil, and leave it for 2 to 4 minutes.
Check with a thermometer, or feel the flask: lukewarm to the touch is about 40°C.
Taste again as it cools in the glass. The same sake often changes noticeably every few degrees.
### My advice
Pour the same kimoto or yamahai at two temperatures side by side: one cool and one at about 45°C. That is the quickest way to discover how much warmth can add. When you shop on sakedeal.com, look for 生酛 (kimoto) or 山廃 (yamahai) on the label if you want a sake to warm. Choose an aromatic ginjo for the fridge.
