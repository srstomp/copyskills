## Facts

F1: Yuzusake (yuzushu) is Japanese sake or shōchū mixed with the juice of yuzu, a fragrant citrus fruit. It tastes tart, floral and a little bitter. It is not a lemon. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts oneLine)
F2: Brewers press fresh yuzu and blend the juice into finished sake, sometimes with a little sugar. Some, like Kozaemon, use a junmai base for a drier, rice-forward finish; others go sweeter and juicier. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts chapter what)
F3: Strength is usually 7-14% alcohol, and it is best served cold or with soda. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts strength.none)
F4: Serve it straight from the fridge at 5-10 °C, neat in a small glass or over ice (one big cube or a full glass). Once opened, keep the bottle in the fridge; it keeps about a month. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts chapter cold)
F5: The mix is 1 part yuzusake to 2 parts soda water, with lots of ice; the shop calls it the yuzu highball: a tall glass full of ice, topped up with soda. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts chapter mix and mix.pours)
F6: Over one big ice cube, the drink opens up as the ice melts. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts mix.pours)
F7: The sparkling one is served fridge-cold in a flute, with no ice and no soda needed. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts mix.pours)
F8: Food: anything fried (bitterballen included), sushi and sashimi, goat's cheese, lemon tart. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts chapter taste)
F9: Yuzu smells floral, with mandarin and pine; it tastes tart with a bitter peel finish; a fruit gives very little juice; it grows in Japan (Kōchi), Korea and China. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/guide.ts taste.traits)
F10: Kozaemon Yuzu: best as a highball 1:2; also on the rocks over one big cube, or neat from the fridge at 5-10 °C in a small glass. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/bottles.ts koz serves)
F11: Heiwa Yuzu: best neat, fridge-cold in a small glass; also a highball 1:2 (the house serve), or on the rocks over one big cube. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/bottles.ts heiwa serves)
F12: Yoigokochi Yuzu: best as a highball 1:2; also on the rocks over one big cube, or neat from the fridge in a small glass. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/bottles.ts ota serves)
F13: Hana Awaka Yuzu (sparkling, 5%, 250 ml, Ozeki, Hyogo): best neat in a flute; also in a wine glass, fridge-cold; serve it fridge-cold with no ice and no soda. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/bottles.ts ozeki serves, serveNote)
F14: Salt'n'Yuzu (yuzu and salt on a junmai base, Kitajima Shuzō, Shiga): serve at 1-10 °C, on the rocks or with soda, as an aperitif or a digestif. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/bottles.ts salt serves, serveNote)
F15: Seifuku Yuzu (yuzu and shikuwasa in awamori, Ishigaki, Okinawa): on the rocks or with soda; the shop's moment is a hearty meal. (source: /Users/steve/Projects/sakebase/platform/apps/yuzusake/src/content/nl/bottles.ts seifuku serves, moment)
