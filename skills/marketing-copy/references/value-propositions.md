# Value Propositions Reference

Formulas, patterns, and a testing checklist for writing value propositions. Load alongside `../../persuasion-frameworks/references/fab.md` which provides the Features-Advantages-Benefits structure for building the underlying argument.

---

## Classic Formula

**"We help [audience] [achieve goal] by [method] unlike [alternative]."**

This formula forces four decisions: who you serve, what they get, how you deliver it, and why you're different from what they'd otherwise use. Every vague value prop is vague because it dodges at least one of these four.

| Slot | What to put here | Common mistake |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| [audience] | Specific segment, not everyone | "businesses" or "teams" -- too broad |
| [achieve goal] | Measurable outcome or named relief | "grow faster" -- no specificity |
| [method] | The mechanism that makes it real | Skipping this entirely |
| [alternative] | What they'd use without you | "the competition" -- name the actual alternative |

### Examples using the formula

> "We help B2B SaaS companies reduce churn by making renewal conversations predictable, unlike CRM reports that only show you who left after it's too late."

> "We help independent consultants send client-ready proposals in under 10 minutes, by turning rough notes into formatted documents, unlike starting from a blank template every time."

> "We help D2C brands recover abandoned carts through behavioral email sequences, unlike generic blast campaigns that hit every subscriber with the same message."

## Positioning Statement Template

For internal use and brand strategy. More structured than the classic formula.

```
For [target customer]
Who [has this problem or need]
[Product name] is a [product category]
That [primary benefit / key differentiator]
Unlike [primary alternative]
Our product [specific proof point or capability]
```

**Example:**

```
For mid-market marketing teams
Who spend more time pulling reports than acting on them
Attributer is a revenue attribution platform
That connects every form fill to its original ad source in real time
Unlike spreadsheet-based attribution or last-click GA4 reports
Our platform ties campaign spend directly to closed-won revenue in your CRM
```

## Differentiation Patterns

Three ways to frame what makes the product different. Pick the one that's most defensible and specific for the brief.

### Feature-based differentiation

Lead with a capability competitors don't have. Only works when the feature is genuinely unique and the audience cares about it.

Pattern: "[Product] is the only [category] that [specific capability]."
> "Loom is the only video messaging tool that lets you record, edit, and share inside the same browser tab -- no app install, no account required for viewers."

### Outcome-based differentiation

Lead with a result that competitors can't credibly claim. Strongest when backed by data.

Pattern: "[Audience] using [Product] [specific measurable result] in [timeframe]."
> "Sales teams using Gong close deals 21 days faster on average, because every call gets analyzed for the language patterns that actually move buyers."

### Experience-based differentiation

Lead with how it feels to use the product vs. alternatives. Strongest for consumer products and UX-first tools.

Pattern: "[Doing hard thing] without [the thing that makes it hard]."
> "File taxes without spending a weekend decoding IRS forms. TurboTax asks plain-language questions and fills everything in for you."

## Value Proposition Testing Checklist

Run every value prop through these four questions before using it.

**Is it specific?**
Can you point to a concrete outcome, timeframe, number, or named behavior? If the answer is "we help companies grow," it's not specific. Find the metric or the mechanism.

**Is it unique?**
Could your three closest competitors publish this exact claim without changing a word? If yes, it's a category claim, not a differentiator. Find the thing only you can say.

**Is it believable?**
Does the claim match what a skeptical buyer would accept without proof? If it requires a leap of faith, add a proof point. A specific customer result or a mechanism explanation makes a claim believable. An adjective doesn't.

**Is it relevant?**
Does the person reading this care about the outcome you're promising? A value prop can be specific, unique, and believable and still miss if it addresses a problem the buyer doesn't feel. Confirm the problem from the brief before choosing which benefit to lead with.

## Value Proposition Examples

### B2B SaaS: RevOps tool

> "RevOps teams using Clari cut forecasting prep from 6 hours a week to 40 minutes -- because every rep's pipeline data flows in automatically, without a single manual update."

Differentiation type: Outcome-based. Specific (6 hours to 40 minutes), mechanism named (automatic data flow).

### E-commerce: Apparel brand

> "Clothes that fit like they were made for you -- because they were. Proportion-fit sizing for women between standard sizes, with no alterations needed."

Differentiation type: Experience-based. Names the exact audience, names the problem (between standard sizes), states the specific relief (no alterations).

### Consumer app: Personal finance

> "See every account, every transaction, every subscription you forgot about -- in one place, updated live. YNAB users save an average of $600 in their first two months."

Differentiation type: Outcome-based with feature support. Specific result ($600 in two months), feature named as mechanism (one place, live updates).

### Professional services: Recruiting firm

> "We fill senior engineering roles in an average of 23 days. Every candidate has cleared a technical screen before you see their name -- so you review three, not thirty."

Differentiation type: Outcome-based (23 days), experience-based (three candidates not thirty). Named mechanism: pre-vetted pipeline.
