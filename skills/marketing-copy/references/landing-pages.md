# Landing Pages Reference

Structural patterns and rules for landing pages, hero sections, and CTAs. Load this file alongside the relevant framework reference (AIDA, PASTOR, or 4Ps) before drafting.

---

## Hero Section Patterns

Three proven configurations. Pick based on what proof the brief provides.

**Pattern A: Headline + Subhead + CTA**
Best when: the offer is clear and the audience already feels the problem.
```
[Headline: bold claim or problem statement]
[Subhead: one sentence expanding the claim with a specific benefit]
[CTA button: action verb + what they get]
```
> "Your proposals take too long to write.
> Paste your notes and get a client-ready proposal in under 10 minutes.
> Write My First Proposal Free"

**Pattern B: Headline + Visual + CTA**
Best when: the product is visual or the transformation is best shown.
```
[Headline: outcome the image illustrates]
[CTA button: action verb + low-commitment language]
[Supporting visual: before/after, product screenshot, or social proof stat]
```
> "See exactly where your funnel leaks.
> Try It Free for 14 Days
> [Screenshot of dashboard highlighting drop-off points]"

**Pattern C: Headline + Social Proof + CTA**
Best when: the offer is credible but the audience is skeptical.
```
[Headline: direct benefit claim]
[Social proof: specific number, customer quote, or recognizable logo strip]
[CTA button: action verb + what happens next]
```
> "Cut reporting time by 3 hours a week.
> Used by 2,400 marketing teams at companies including Shopify, HubSpot, and Clearbit.
> Start Free Trial"

## Standard Page Flow

This sequence applies to most landing pages. Short-form pages compress it; long-form pages expand each section.

| Section | Purpose | Key rule |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Hero | Establish the offer and hook the reader | Must contain headline, value prop, primary CTA |
| Problem / Pain | Name the cost of inaction | Specific, not generic. One problem per section. |
| Solution | Introduce the product as the fix | Don't list features yet. State the transformation. |
| Features / Benefits | Support the solution with specifics | FAB order: feature then what it does then why it matters |
| Social Proof | Make the claim believable | Numbers, names, and specifics beat logos and generic quotes |
| CTA | Ask for the action | Repeat the primary CTA. Add urgency or risk-reversal. |
| FAQ | Remove the last objections | Address 3-5 real purchase blockers. Skip fake FAQs. |

Long-form pages (SaaS free trial, high-consideration purchase, PASTOR structure) can add a Founder/Story section between Solution and Features, and a Comparison section between Features and Social Proof.

## Above-the-Fold Rules

Everything visible without scrolling must do three things:

1. **Communicate the headline** -- what this product does or what problem it solves. One sentence. No clever ambiguity.
2. **State the value proposition** -- who it's for and what they get. Can be headline or subhead.
3. **Show the primary CTA** -- one button or link. Specific action verb. Never "click here" or "learn more."

Things that do NOT belong above the fold: navigation-heavy headers, feature lists, video backgrounds with no fallback text, testimonials without context, or pricing tables.

## Section-by-Section Patterns

### Problem / Pain section

Name the exact problem, not the category. Readers skip sections that describe a category they're in. They stop for sections that name the specific frustration they felt this morning.

Avoid: "Marketing teams struggle with attribution."
Write: "You ran four campaigns last quarter. You still can't tell your CFO which one drove revenue."

One problem per section. If you have three problems, give each its own short section.

### Solution section

Introduce the product as the fix for the named problem. Do not list features here. State the transformation.

Template: "[Product] gives [audience] [outcome] by [mechanism]."
> "Attributer gives B2B marketing teams revenue clarity by connecting every form fill to its original ad source -- even across multi-touch journeys."

### Features / Benefits section

Use FAB order within each feature block. Three to five features maximum on a standard landing page.

```
Feature name (short label)
What it does (one sentence, product behavior)
Why it matters (one sentence, reader outcome)
```

> **Live revenue attribution**
> Connects each contact to its first-touch and last-touch source in real time.
> Your team stops arguing about which channel works and starts doubling down on what's actually driving deals.

### Social Proof section

Hierarchy of proof, strongest to weakest:

1. Specific metric from a named customer ("Reduced churn 34% in 90 days -- Lena Park, Head of Retention, Segment")
2. Named customer quote with context ("We cut onboarding from 3 weeks to 4 days")
3. Aggregate stat from real data ("Rated 4.8 out of 5 by 1,200 reviews on G2")
4. Logo strip (shows company type; provides no specifics)

Never fabricate a testimonial. Never use anonymous quotes if named quotes are available.

### CTA section (closing)

Repeat the primary CTA from the hero. Add one of:
- Risk-reversal ("No credit card required", "Cancel any time", "30-day money-back guarantee")
- Urgency if real ("Cohort starts May 12 -- 8 spots left")
- Secondary CTA for the not-ready reader ("Book a demo" alongside "Start free trial")

## Common Landing Page Mistakes

**Writing to everyone.** Landing pages that try to speak to multiple audiences speak to none of them. One page, one reader, one job.

**Hero without a CTA.** The most common structural failure. If someone reads the hero and wants to act, there should be a button waiting for them.

**Features without benefits.** "14-day free trial" is a feature. "Try every feature for free before spending a dollar" is a benefit. Translate.

**Social proof that proves nothing.** A logo strip tells the reader your product exists. A specific result from a specific person tells them it works. Use specifics.

**FAQ that avoids real objections.** Real purchase blockers: pricing, data security, switching cost, integration complexity, contract terms. FAQs that answer "how do I log in?" waste the section.

**Too many CTAs competing for attention.** Every CTA after the first one dilutes conversion. One primary action per page. Secondary CTAs only when they serve a different intent (e.g., "book a demo" for people not ready to buy).
