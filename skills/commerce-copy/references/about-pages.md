# About and Brand Pages

About pages, "who we are", team and brand-story pages. For shops, these pages carry trust: an SEO audit will flag an anonymous shop, especially for age-restricted or high-value products.

## What the page must say, from facts only

1. **Who runs the shop:** real names, roles and any credential, exactly as the fact sheet gives them. A sommelier, buyer or founder with a stated credential is the strongest trust signal the page has.
2. **The legal entity:** company name, registration and address as given; usually a short line.
3. **What the shop does differently,** in facts: where it sources, how it selects, how it ships and stores, which related shops or brands belong to the same company.
4. **How to reach a person:** email, phone, address, response times, as given.

Never invent a person, a credential, an origin story, a founding year, a mission statement or a quote. An About page with fewer, true facts is better than a full one with invented warmth. Missing items go to `needs` ("Name and one line about the sake sommelier", "Year the shop started").

## Voice

Write in the first person plural ("we") only if the brand's voice samples do. Avoid the stock About moves: "Our story began with a simple idea", "We're passionate about", "Our mission is to", "At [Shop], we believe". State what the people do.

## Bylines

When a person with a credential stands behind content (tasting notes, guides), the brief may ask for a byline: "Tasting notes by [name], [credential]". Use the name and credential exactly as given, and only on content that person actually wrote or approved, according to the brief.
