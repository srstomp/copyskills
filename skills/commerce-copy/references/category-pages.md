# Category Pages

Intros and H1s for category, collection, style or region pages ("Umeshu", "Sparkling sake", "Sake from Niigata").

## What the intro is for

A category page is where a searcher lands from a query like "umeshu kopen" or "sparkling sake". The product grid below the intro does the selling. The intro does three things, in this order:
1. **Says what the category is**, in one or two sentences, for a reader who may not know (umeshu is a liqueur made by steeping ume in alcohol with sugar). General knowledge is allowed only at this level and only when the brief allows it.
2. **Says what this shop's range looks like**, from the fact sheet: how many products, where they come from, the spread of styles, sizes or prices. Only facts on the sheet.
3. **Helps the reader choose within it**: which product for which use or taste, with links (product names) where the brief asks for them.

Skip "Welcome to our X collection", "Discover our range of", and any sentence that would fit every category page on the site.

## H1 and query

- The H1 names the category in the words searchers use, in the locale's language: "Umeshu kopen" is fine as an H1 on a Dutch shop when the brief targets that query; "Umeshu" alone is fine too. Do not stuff a second keyword into it.
- Put the target query in the title tag, and use it once in the first two sentences of the intro if it reads naturally. If it does not read naturally in a sentence (most "X kopen" queries do not), leave it to the title and H1.

## Length and uniqueness

Follow the brief. Every category intro on a site must be unique: a shared template with the category name swapped in is the failure the audit is flagging. If the shop has only two or three products in a category, say so plainly and keep the intro short; do not inflate a small range.

## Missing products

If the brief says the category has products the fact sheet does not describe, do not write a placeholder sentence about them ("There is also a third umeshu in our range"). Leave them out of the prose, and return a `needs` item.
