# Product Pages

How to write a product description and the small copy around it (serve tips, pairing lines, alt text) for a web shop.

## What a buyer needs from the description

A shop page already shows the name, price, size, stock and often a spec table. The description adds what those cannot:
1. **What it is like.** Taste, smell, texture, or for non-food products what it does and how it feels in use, in plain words a buyer recognises. Use the fact sheet's tasting notes; do not add your own.
2. **How to use it.** For drinks: temperature, glass, with or without ice or soda, how long it keeps once opened. For other products: the one or two uses that matter.
3. **What sets it apart.** The facts from Step 2 of the skill: the maker, the place, a method, a rice or ingredient, a price per glass. Only facts on the sheet.
4. **What to eat it with** or what it goes with, if the sheet has pairings.

Lead with 1 or 3, whichever is stronger for this product. A description that opens with the maker's founding year is a history lesson; open with it only if the history is the point of the product.

## Length

The brief sets the budget. A word floor from an SEO audit (such as "about 150 unique words") is a coverage target, not a quota: reach it with more true facts about this product (serve, food, origin, maker), never with adjectives, restated specs or generic category text. If the sheet runs out first, stop and add a `needs` item.

## The page as one text

The description, the serve tip, the pairing lines and any short "tagline" are read together. They must agree with each other and with the spec table:
- The serve tip repeats the best serve, not a secondary one. If the best serve is "neat, fridge-cold", the tip does not suggest soda.
- A pairing line written once per dish must not repeat one sentence across dishes ("Classic izakaya staples." five times is a template). Write one line per dish that says why it works, or one line for the group.
- Do not restate in prose what the spec table shows, except in a meta description.

## Names

- Use the product name exactly as the fact sheet gives it.
- If the product is also sold under a trade name or label name that buyers search for (given in the brief or fact sheet), mention it once in the description, naturally ("sold elsewhere as Tsuru Ume Yuzu"), and suggest it for the title tag and structured data in `decisions`.
- Romanisation of foreign names follows the shop's existing pages (Shuzō or Shuzo, the same everywhere on the page).

## Image alt text

Describe what the image shows, for someone who cannot see it, in 10 to 125 characters: "Green 720 ml bottle of Heiwa Yuzu with a white label". Not a keyword list, not "image of". The product name belongs in it when the product is in the picture.

## Example

Facts (excerpt): F1 dry honjozo, 720 ml, 16%. F4 brewery founded 1872 in Kakegawa, Shizuoka. F6 tasting: melon blossom, green apple, steamed rice; light to medium body; dry, short finish. F7 serve at 15 °C, 20 °C or warmed to about 40 °C; warm brings out savoury grain notes. F8 food: grilled fish, shio yakitori, gyoza.

Before (template and slop):
> Experience the timeless elegance of this exceptional honjozo. Crisp, clean and refined, it dances on the palate with notes of melon and apple before a bone-dry finish. Perfect for any occasion, it's not just a sake, it's a journey to Japan.

After:
> Melon blossom and green apple on the nose, steamed rice underneath, and a dry, short finish. Drink it cool, at 15 to 20 °C, or warm it to about 40 °C, which brings out savoury grain notes. It goes with grilled fish, salted yakitori and gyoza. The brewery has made sake in Kakegawa, Shizuoka, since 1872.

Every claim in the "after" maps to F1 to F8. Nothing is added: no "elegant", no reaction the writer cannot know, and no cause and effect the sheet does not give (the sheet says what warming does; it says nothing about what 15 °C does, so the copy does not either).
