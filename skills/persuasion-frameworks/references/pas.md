# PAS: Problem-Agitate-Solve

**Full name:** Problem-Agitate-Solve
**Best for:** Cold outreach, email marketing, direct response copy
**Strength:** When the audience has a clear pain point and is already aware of it

---

## Framework Steps

### 1. Problem

Name the reader's problem directly and specifically. The problem statement should feel like the reader wrote it themselves. Vague problems ("struggling with productivity") do not land. Specific problems ("spending two hours a day on status updates that nobody reads") create recognition. Recognition earns trust.

The Problem step is not about introducing new information. It is about demonstrating that you understand the reader's situation accurately enough to be trusted. Write from the reader's perspective, not the product's.

Test your Problem statement: if the reader could forward it to a colleague and say "this is exactly what I am dealing with," you have hit the right level of specificity.

### 2. Agitate

Intensify the problem before offering relief. Show what happens if the problem goes unsolved -- the downstream consequences, the compounding costs, the things the reader quietly worries about.

Agitation is not fearmongering. It is helping the reader feel the full weight of a problem they have learned to tolerate. Many pain points become background noise over time. The Agitate step brings that noise back to the foreground.

The goal is not to scare the reader. It is to make the problem feel worth solving now, rather than after more months of tolerating it. Two to three specific downstream consequences are usually enough.

Do not agitate problems the reader does not already feel. If you need to convince someone they have a problem, PAS is the wrong framework -- the Agitate step assumes problem awareness.

### 3. Solve

Present the solution as the direct answer to everything raised in the Problem and Agitate steps. The Solve should feel inevitable -- as if you built it specifically for the problem you just described.

The Solve should address the root cause raised in Problem, not just the surface symptoms. If the Problem was "manual reconciliation wastes 14 hours a month," the Solve should explain specifically how that goes away -- not just "we automate your finance work."

Close with a clear, low-friction CTA that matches the sales cycle stage. Cold email CTA: request a 20-minute call. Product page CTA: start a trial. The ask should be proportional to the trust built in the copy.

---

## Format Notes

PAS is compact by design. It works in short-form (a cold email of 150 words) and long-form (a sales page with a detailed Problem-Agitate section and a multi-paragraph Solve). The structure scales.

In short-form copy, each section is often one to three sentences. The Problem names the situation, the Agitate names one specific consequence, and the Solve presents the mechanism and CTA.

In longer direct response copy, the Agitate section can be extended with multiple downstream consequences, stories from people in the problem state, or data quantifying the cost. The Solve section can then run through the solution in detail, include social proof, and end with a CTA. The ratio of Problem to Agitate to Solve tends to be roughly 20/40/40 in long-form copy.

## Examples

### Example 1 -- B2B SaaS: expense reporting tool (cold outreach email)

**Problem:** Finance teams at mid-size companies spend an average of 14 hours a month reconciling expense reports manually -- spreadsheets, receipts forwarded by email, approvals tracked in Slack.

**Agitate:** The real cost is not the hours. It is the errors. A single misclassified expense can cascade through your books at quarter close. And when auditors ask for backup documentation, those Slack threads and email attachments from three months ago are effectively gone.

**Solve:** Expensify Pro connects to your card feeds, categorizes automatically, and routes approvals through a single dashboard. Reconciliation drops from days to hours. Every receipt is stored, searchable, and audit-ready. Book a 20-minute demo this week and we will show you the setup for a company your size.

---

### Example 2 -- Consumer: meal kit subscription (display ad)

**Problem:** You plan to cook. Then Wednesday happens and you order delivery again.

**Agitate:** It is not a discipline problem. It is a planning problem. By the time you figure out what to make, check what you have, and build a grocery list, takeout already won. That decision costs you -- in money, in how you feel the next morning, and in the slow drift away from eating the way you actually want to.

**Solve:** Green Chef delivers pre-portioned ingredients and 15-minute recipes every week. No planning. No grocery runs. Dinner is on the table before you second-guess it. First box is 60% off.

---

### Example 3 -- Professional services: freelance accountant targeting small business owners (prospecting email)

**Problem:** You started your business to do the work you are good at. Instead, you are spending Sunday nights in QuickBooks.

**Agitate:** Every hour on bookkeeping is an hour not spent on client work. Worse: DIY bookkeeping has a real error rate. Misclassified expenses, missed deductions, late filings -- the penalties compound and the IRS does not care that you were busy with clients.

**Solve:** I handle bookkeeping, quarterly estimates, and annual filings for service businesses with $250K-$1M in revenue. Fixed monthly fee, no surprises. Reply with your industry and I will send you a scope and a price in 24 hours.

## Common Mistakes

- **Agitating a problem the reader does not already feel.** PAS only works when the problem is already recognized. If you need to convince the reader they have the problem first, use AIDA or QUEST to build awareness before agitating.

- **Over-agitating.** Piling on too much pain makes copy feel manipulative or exhausting. Two to three downstream consequences are enough. Stop before the reader feels lectured or patronized.

- **Solve that does not match the problem.** Raising a specific operational problem (manual reconciliation) and then solving with a vague benefit ("transform your business") breaks the logical frame. The solve must address exactly what was raised.

- **Skipping the agitate step.** Going directly from Problem to Solve turns PAS into a flat feature announcement. The agitation step is what creates the emotional urgency that makes the solution feel necessary.

- **Problem written from the company's perspective.** "Many businesses struggle with X" is not PAS. Write to the reader: "You are probably dealing with X right now." First and second person keep the problem feeling personal.

---

## When NOT to Use

- **Warm leads or existing customers.** Agitating pain with someone already in your pipeline or already using your product can feel aggressive or tone-deaf. Use AIDA or FAB with warm audiences, which lead with value rather than pain.

- **Aspirational, identity-driven products.** Luxury goods, fitness brands, and lifestyle products sell on desire and identity, not pain avoidance. PAS can make those products feel like remedies for problems rather than objects of aspiration. AIDA or BAB are better fits.

- **Audiences who are not yet problem-aware.** PAS assumes problem awareness. If the reader does not yet know they have the problem you are describing, the agitation step will confuse rather than compel. AIDA or QUEST, which build awareness gradually, are more appropriate.
