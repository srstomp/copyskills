# FAB: Features-Advantages-Benefits

**Full name:** Features-Advantages-Benefits
**Best for:** Product descriptions, comparison pages, technical audiences
**Strength:** Connects what the product does to why the reader should care. Especially effective when the audience evaluates on specs but buys on outcomes.

---

## Framework Steps

### 1. Feature

State what the product has or does. Features are objective and factual: specifications, capabilities, materials, components. A feature answers "what is it?"

Features alone do not persuade. Most readers cannot immediately translate specs into personal value without help. A reader who sees "32GB RAM" may not know whether that matters for their use case. FAB does the translation work for them.

In B2B copy, technical buyers often need features stated precisely before they trust the advantages and benefits. Do not skip or soften the feature step for technical audiences.

### 2. Advantage

Explain what the feature means in practical terms. The advantage answers "so what does that actually do?"

The Advantage bridges the gap between spec and real-world behavior. It is still product-focused, describing how the feature behaves rather than attributing the feature. Advantages are neutral statements about capability, not persuasive claims about reader outcomes -- those come next.

Weak advantage: "our battery is larger than competitors." Strong advantage: "the battery lasts through a full workday and a four-hour flight without needing a charge."

### 3. Benefit

Connect the feature and advantage to the reader's life, work, or goals. The benefit answers "what does this mean for me?"

Benefits are reader-focused. They describe an outcome the reader wants: time saved, risk reduced, status gained, frustration eliminated. Benefits are where the purchase decision is made.

Translate from product language to reader language. "40% reduction in import time" (advantage) becomes "your analysts get to work two hours earlier every Monday" (benefit). The benefit names the human outcome, not the performance metric.

## Examples

### Example 1 -- E-commerce: noise-cancelling headphones (product description)

**Feature:** 40-hour battery with USB-C fast charge -- 10 minutes charges 3 hours of playback.

**Advantage:** The headphones outlast a full international flight and a full workday. A short charge before leaving the office covers the evening commute and a gym session.

**Benefit:** You stop rationing listening time. You stop carrying cables. The headphones are ready when you are.

---

**Feature:** Four-microphone array with AI voice isolation.

**Advantage:** Background noise -- keyboard clicks, coffee shop chatter, street noise -- is separated from your voice signal before it reaches the call.

**Benefit:** You sound professional on every call regardless of where you are working. No more muting yourself between sentences or apologizing for background noise mid-conversation.

---

### Example 2 -- B2B SaaS: CRM platform (comparison page)

**Feature:** Native two-way sync with Gmail and Outlook, updating in real time.

**Advantage:** Every email thread, reply, meeting invite, and attachment logs automatically to the contact record without any manual data entry from the sales rep.

**Benefit:** Sales reps spend zero time on CRM hygiene. Managers get accurate pipeline data without chasing their team for updates before the weekly call.

---

**Feature:** Role-based permissions with field-level access controls.

**Advantage:** Admins control exactly which users can see which fields. Compensation data, contract terms, and custom fields can be restricted without hiding the full contact record.

**Benefit:** Your team works in one system without compliance or confidentiality concerns. No more exporting sensitive data into separate spreadsheets that fall out of sync.

---

### Example 3 -- Services: managed cloud hosting (technical audience landing page)

**Feature:** 99.99% uptime SLA backed by multi-region failover across three availability zones in different geographic regions.

**Advantage:** If one data center experiences a failure, traffic routes automatically to a healthy region with no manual intervention and no single point of failure in the architecture.

**Benefit:** Your application stays up during incidents that would otherwise require a 2am emergency response. Your engineering team spends time on planned improvements, not unplanned outages.

## Common Mistakes

- **Stopping at features.** Writing a spec sheet and calling it a product description. Most readers cannot translate specs into personal value on their own -- that translation is the work FAB exists to do.

- **Advantages that are still features in disguise.** "Our battery lasts longer than competitors" is a feature comparison, not an advantage. The advantage describes what the feature actually does in practice: "lasts through a full conference day without needing a charge."

- **Benefits that are vague.** "Save time and money" is not a benefit -- it is a placeholder. Quantify or make concrete: "cut reporting preparation from three hours to twenty minutes" is a benefit. "Saves time" is a claim.

- **Using FAB for every feature when only a few matter.** Long FAB chains dilute the important claims. Identify the two or three features that most directly drive the purchase decision for your specific audience and go deep on those.

- **Benefits written from the company's perspective.** "Our customers love this feature" is not a benefit statement. Translate to the reader: "You will be able to..." The benefit belongs to the reader, not the vendor.

- **Reversing the order.** Starting with benefit and working back to feature feels like backfilling justification. The Feature-Advantage-Benefit sequence follows a logical discovery arc that mirrors how a thoughtful buyer evaluates a product.

---

## When NOT to Use

- **Narrative or brand-led copy.** FAB is analytical. When the goal is emotional connection -- brand storytelling, founder narratives, cause-based marketing -- FAB's feature-first structure is too transactional for the context. Use BAB or Star-Story-Solution instead.

- **Cold outreach to audiences who do not know they have the problem.** FAB assumes the reader is already evaluating a solution. If they have not yet recognized the problem, lead with PAS to establish the problem before presenting features and benefits.

- **Highly constrained short-form copy.** FAB requires enough space to articulate all three layers for each key feature. In ad headlines, SMS, or social copy with tight word counts, use AIDA or a single benefit statement rather than a compressed FAB structure.
