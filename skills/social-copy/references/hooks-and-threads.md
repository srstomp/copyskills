# Hooks and Threads

Hook formulas for scroll-stopping first lines, thread structures, and opening line patterns by platform. Load this file when writing threads, carousels, or any content where the opening line is the primary lever.

---

## Hook Formulas

The hook is the first line (or first 1-3 seconds) of any social content. Its only job is to make the audience stop and continue. A good hook creates a gap -- between what the reader knows and what they want to know -- that can only be closed by reading further.

### Pattern Interrupt

Break the expected pattern of the feed. If every post in a category opens with a professional insight, open with a confession, a number, or a bare statement of fact.

Examples:
- "I was wrong about cold outreach for 3 years."
- "We lost $40,000 on a single ad campaign. Here's what happened."
- "Nobody talks about the part that actually takes the longest."

Use when: The topic is familiar and the audience is saturated with similar content.

### Bold Claim

State something provocative, counterintuitive, or at odds with conventional wisdom. Must be defensible in the body of the post.

Examples:
- "Most onboarding sequences make churn worse."
- "Your email subject line matters less than you think."
- "Consistency is overrated. Here's what actually builds an audience."

Use when: You have a specific, well-supported point that challenges a common assumption.

### Question

Ask something specific that the target reader is actively wondering about. Generic questions ("Have you ever thought about...?") do not work. The question must be specific to a real tension the reader has right now.

Examples:
- "What do you do when a client ghosts after the proposal?"
- "Why do some SaaS companies grow with zero marketing budget?"
- "How do you write a cold email that doesn't read like a cold email?"

Use when: The question maps to something the audience is actively searching for or wrestling with.

### Stat Lead

Open with a number that is surprising, counterintuitive, or creates a knowledge gap. Source should be real and, if possible, cited in the body.

Examples:
- "73% of B2B buyers complete their research before talking to a salesperson."
- "The average SaaS customer churns within 90 days of onboarding. Not 12 months."
- "We analyzed 400 landing pages. 61% had a CTA above the fold that said 'Learn More.'"

Use when: You have a specific, verifiable stat and can explain what it means.

### Story Open

Start in the middle of a scene. No setup, no context. Drop the reader directly into the action.

Examples:
- "The client called on a Tuesday and said they'd found someone else."
- "We had 72 hours to rewrite the entire onboarding flow before launch."
- "I sent the email to the wrong list. 14,000 people."

Use when: You have a real story with a specific inciting moment.

## Thread Structures

### X/Twitter: Numbered Hook + Payoff

Best for: tutorials, listicles, research breakdowns, frameworks.

Structure:
- Tweet 1: hook + specific promise ("I studied 100 X accounts that grew 10k followers in 6 months. Here's what they all did differently:")
- Tweets 2-N: one point per tweet, each self-contained and numbered
- Final tweet: key takeaway + CTA (follow, share, reply, or link)

Rules:
- Each tweet must work standalone if screenshotted
- No padding. If a point needs two tweets, split the point more cleanly
- Do not number tweet 1. Number from tweet 2 onward (1/, 2/, 3/)

### X/Twitter: Story Arc Thread

Best for: personal narratives, case studies, opinion pieces.

Structure:
- Tweet 1: the hook moment (not "let me tell you my story" -- start in the action)
- Tweets 2-4: complication, tension, or what happened next
- Tweets 5-7: turning point or revelation
- Final tweet: lesson, reframe, or implication + optional CTA

Rules:
- Resist explaining too early. Let the tension build before the lesson
- Keep each tweet at 200-250 characters for readability
- Do not add a number format. Story threads flow better unnumbered

### LinkedIn: Story Arc with Lesson

Best for: professional insights, career moments, business lessons.

Structure:
- Line 1: hook (the moment, the fact, the counterintuitive claim) -- this is what earns the "see more" click
- Lines 2-5: the setup or context in short, line-broken sentences
- Lines 6-10: the tension, complication, or turning point
- Lines 11-14: the resolution or lesson
- Final lines: explicit takeaway + optional question or CTA

Formatting rules:
- One sentence (sometimes two) per line break
- No wall-of-text paragraphs
- Each line break functions like a scroll reward
- The hook must stand alone above the fold

### Instagram: Carousel Slide-by-Slide Flow

Best for: educational content, how-to guides, listicles, before/after transformations.

Structure:
- Slide 1 (Hook): bold claim or question that makes the viewer swipe. Include as few words as possible. The visual carries the weight here; the copy is the headline.
- Slides 2-6 (Body): one idea per slide. Short heading, 1-2 supporting sentences max. Each slide should make sense if someone screenshots it.
- Second-to-last slide (Proof or Summary): key takeaway, stat, or before/after result
- Last slide (CTA): one clear action (save this, follow for more, link in bio, share with someone who needs this)

Rules:
- Assume the viewer reads only slide 1 and the last slide. Both must work independently.
- Keep text minimal. If a slide needs more than 40 words, split it.
- Consistent visual template per slide (color, font, layout) increases completion rate.

## Opening Line Patterns That Work Per Platform

### LinkedIn
- Direct confession: "I made a mistake that cost us our biggest client."
- Counterintuitive fact: "Cold outreach has a 93% failure rate. We use it anyway."
- Bare statement with tension: "I almost quit consulting last year."
- Avoid: "I'm excited to announce", "In today's world", "Lessons learned"

### X/Twitter
- Short declarative: "Most SEO advice is backwards."
- Specific number + implication: "200 replies to a 47-word tweet. Here's what it said:"
- Confession or reversal: "I deleted 6 months of content and started over."
- Avoid: "Agree or disagree?", "Thoughts?", generic motivational openers

### Instagram
- Curiosity gap before truncation: "This mistake is killing your engagement (and it's not what you think)"
- Specific result: "800 to 14,000 followers in 4 months. Full breakdown:"
- Direct address: "If your reels are getting views but no followers, read this."
- Avoid: "Hey loves!", product announcements with no hook

### TikTok
- Caption hook mirrors video hook (they reinforce each other, never repeat)
- Specific promise: "3-step process I use to write a week of content in 45 minutes"
- Challenge/contrast: "Everyone says X. Actually it's Y."
- Avoid: vague hooks, hashtag-only captions
