# Engagement Patterns

CTA patterns, engagement drivers, community voice guidance, and posting frequency by platform. Load this file when writing engagement-focused content, community replies, or when the brief specifies driving interaction rather than awareness or conversion.

---

## CTA Patterns Per Platform

A CTA on social content is not the same as a CTA on a landing page. Social CTAs invite participation, not just action. The goal is a behavior the platform rewards (comment, share, save, click) framed as something the audience actually wants to do.

### LinkedIn
- "Agree? Drop your take in the comments."
- "What's your experience with this? I'd genuinely like to know."
- "If this was useful, share it with someone building their first [X]."
- "Save this if you're planning to [relevant task] soon."
- "What would you add to this list?"

Avoid: "Like and subscribe", "Follow me for more content", corporate calls-to-action that sound like they came from a marketing slide.

### X/Twitter
- Quote retweet bait: "Share this and tag someone who needs to see it."
- Opinion hook at end: "What's your take? Genuine disagreement welcome."
- Thread CTA on tweet 1: "Sharing 10 things we learned. Retweet if useful."
- For reach: "Like this if you agree. Retweet if you disagree." (creates participation regardless of opinion)
- For community: "Drop a reply with [specific thing] -- compiling the best answers."

Avoid: aggressive follow requests, "RT if you [emotional statement]" manipulation patterns.

### Instagram
- "Save this for when you need it." (highest-value engagement signal)
- "Share this with someone who [specific situation]."
- "Comment your [number/answer] below."
- "Follow for [specific content type] every [day]."
- "Link in bio to get the full [resource]."

Avoid: "Double tap if you agree", vague "let me know your thoughts" with no specificity.

### TikTok
- "Comment [word] and I'll send you the full breakdown."
- "Follow and I'll post part 2 this week."
- "Share this with someone who's been [specific struggle]."
- "Stitch this with your take." (invites response content)

Avoid: begging for follows, "like this video" as a CTA (audiences tune it out).

## Engagement Drivers

These content patterns consistently generate higher engagement across platforms. Use them when the brief prioritizes community interaction over awareness or traffic.

### Questions
Work when they are specific and answerable. "What do you think?" is not a question -- it is avoidance. "What's one thing your onboarding flow is missing?" is a question.

Good question structures:
- "What would you do in this situation: [scenario]?"
- "Have you tried [approach]? What happened?"
- "Which of these is your biggest challenge right now: A or B?"

### Polls
LinkedIn polls: 2-4 options, binary or forced choice works best. Results often spark more comments than the poll itself.

X/Twitter polls: 2-4 options, up to 7 days. Work best when the framing implies the answer is debatable.

Instagram stories polls: yes/no slider or binary choice. High participation, low depth. Good for audience research, not for driving substantive engagement.

### Hot Takes and Contrarian Opinions
These drive comments but must be defensible. A hot take that cannot be supported in the body of the post is just provocation.

Format: State the contrarian position in the hook. Spend the body of the post explaining the specific evidence or reasoning. Invite disagreement explicitly at the end.

Example: "Newsletters are overrated for B2B companies. [Body: specific argument]. Disagree? Tell me why."

Avoid: stating that something is "overrated" or "dead" without specifics. These hooks are common and generate disengagement.

### Contrarian Frame Patterns
- "Everyone says [X]. Here's why that's wrong:"
- "I used to believe [X]. Then [specific thing happened]."
- "The conventional wisdom on [topic] ignores [specific factor]."
- "You don't need [common advice]. You need [specific alternative]."

## Community Voice: Promotional vs. Conversational vs. Educational

The right tone depends on the post's goal and where the audience is in their relationship with the brand or creator.

### Promotional
Use when: announcing a product, service, launch, or offer.

Rules:
- Lead with the outcome for the audience, not the announcement itself
- Specifics beat superlatives ("saves 3 hours per week" beats "saves you so much time")
- One CTA, clearly stated, relevant to the platform
- Promotional posts earn lower organic reach on most platforms. Accept that. Run them anyway when the timing is right.

### Conversational
Use when: community building, replies, engagement farming, brand voice maintenance.

Rules:
- Write the way a person writes, not the way a brand writes
- Ask real questions. Respond to real answers.
- Share genuine opinions. Brands with no opinions are invisible.
- First person ("I", "we") over third person ("the team", "our company")

### Educational
Use when: building authority, providing value, driving saves and shares.

Rules:
- Lead with the specific lesson or framework, not with context about why you're sharing it
- Use numbered lists or clear structure for scanability
- End with an implication or application: "If you're doing [X], this means you should [Y]."
- Educational posts earn the most saves (Instagram, LinkedIn) and reposts (X). Prioritize them in the content mix.

## Posting Frequency Guidance

These are starting points, not rules. Consistency within a sustainable cadence beats high-frequency posting that burns out.

| Platform | Starting cadence | Notes |
|----------|-----------------|-------|
| LinkedIn | 3-5 posts per week | Quality over frequency. One strong post beats five mediocre ones. |
| X/Twitter | 1-3 tweets or 1 thread per day | Higher tolerance for volume; conversations matter as much as original posts |
| Instagram | 4-7 feed posts per week (mix of Reels and carousels) + Stories daily | Feed posts drive growth; Stories maintain connection |
| TikTok | 1-3 videos per day for growth phase, 3-5 per week for maintenance | Algorithm rewards consistency and volume more than any other platform |

Reply and comment time counts as posting. Engaging with others' content on LinkedIn and X drives reach on your own posts through network visibility.
