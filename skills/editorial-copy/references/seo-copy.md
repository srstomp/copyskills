## 1. Search Intent Mapping

Search intent is the primary signal. Identify what the searcher actually wants before writing a word. Framework, structure, and tone all follow from intent.

| Intent type | What the searcher wants | Content format | Example queries |
|-------------|------------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Informational | To learn or understand | Educational article, how-to, explainer | "how does X work", "what is X" |
| Navigational | To find a specific site | Brand page, login, contact | "[brand] login", "[brand] pricing" |
| Commercial | To research before buying | Comparison, review, best-of list | "best X for Y", "X vs Y" |
| Transactional | To buy, sign up, or download | Product page, landing page, sign-up | "buy X", "X free trial" |

Matching intent matters more than keyword density. A 2,000-word educational article will not rank for a transactional query no matter how well-optimized it is.

Before writing: check the SERP. What format dominates? What stage of the buying process is the searcher in? Write the content that matches what is already ranking. Deviation from prevailing format requires exceptional content and significant domain authority to overcome.

## 2. Keyword Integration

Keywords must appear naturally. If a sentence sounds written around a keyword, it was. Rewrite it.

### Placement

Primary keyword should appear in:
- The H1 (page title)
- The first 100 words of the body
- At least one H2
- The meta description
- The URL slug (if controllable)

Secondary keywords should appear in body copy where they fit naturally. Do not force them.

### Density

Target: 1-2% for the primary keyword. Use this as a ceiling, not a target. Write the content first, then check frequency. If the keyword appears 4 times in 1,000 words, add it where it genuinely fits. If it appears 25 times, remove redundant mentions.

### Semantic keywords

Use related terms and natural variants rather than repeating the exact primary keyword. "Cold email" -- also use: "cold outreach", "prospecting emails", "outbound email". Semantic richness signals topical authority more reliably than exact-match repetition.

## 3. Heading Hierarchy

**H1:** One per page. Must include the primary keyword. Should match or closely mirror the title tag. 40-70 characters practical range.

**H2:** Break the page into major sections. Each H2 represents a distinct concept or reader question. Include secondary keywords where natural. 3-7 H2s per 1,500-2,000 words is a typical range.

**H3:** Use under H2s when a section needs internal structure. Do not use H3s without a parent H2. Keep labels specific: "When to use H3 tags" beats "Details".

**Anti-patterns:**
- Do not stuff keywords into headings: "Best project management software for small teams using remote collaboration tools" is a keyword list, not a heading
- Do not skip levels (H1 to H3, no H2)

## 4. Meta Descriptions

The meta description does not affect rankings. It affects click-through rate from the SERP. Treat it as ad copy for the page.

**Length:** 150-160 characters. Google truncates at roughly 160; mobile truncates around 120. Front-load the important information.

**What to include:** (1) the primary keyword naturally placed, (2) a clear statement of what the page delivers, (3) a CTA or implicit reason to click.

> Good example (152 chars): "Learn how to write cold emails that get replies. Practical templates, subject line formulas, and the one mistake most senders make. Free guide inside."

> Weak example: "In this comprehensive guide, we will explore the various strategies and techniques that professionals use to write effective cold emails in today's competitive business landscape." -- 180 characters, vague, passive voice, no CTA.

Every page needs a unique meta description. Do not auto-generate from the first 160 characters of body copy.

## 5. Internal Linking

**For link equity:** Link from high-traffic pages to new content you want to rank. Link from new content to pages you most want to convert readers on.

**For topical authority:** Build hub pages per major topic. Link supporting articles to the hub. Link the hub to its most valuable supporting articles. This cluster structure signals to search engines that the hub is the authority on the topic. A scattered set of isolated posts does not build authority the same way.

**Link placement:** Place the most important conversion link early in the post. 2-5 internal links per 1,000 words is a reasonable range. Do not cluster them in one section.

**Anchor text:** Use descriptive, keyword-adjacent anchor text. Vary it; exact-match anchors used repeatedly trigger over-optimization signals. "Read our guide to cold email subject lines" beats "click here" and beats "cold email subject lines" used five times.

## 6. Over-Optimization Warning

SEO copy that reads like SEO copy fails twice: it alienates readers and triggers algorithmic demotion.

Signs of over-optimization:
- The primary keyword appears in every other sentence
- Headings read like keyword lists
- The phrase "This is where [keyword] comes in" appears anywhere
- Transition phrases like "When it comes to [keyword]..." used as pivots
- The copy sounds written by someone told to include a keyword 15 times

The test: read the draft aloud. Awkward phrasing or keyword repetition that you notice will be noticed by every reader.

Fix: write for a reader. Audit for keywords after. Add where they fit. Remove forced mentions. Google's Helpful Content guidance is explicit: content written primarily for search engines, not humans, is downgraded. The optimization ceiling is always human readability.
