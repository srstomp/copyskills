# Readability Guidelines

Readability affects whether copy gets read, not just whether it's grammatically correct. Lower grade level = wider reach. Higher sentence length variance = more human. This file provides targets by audience and copy type.

---

## Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level Targets

Grade level estimates the US school grade needed to comfortably read the text. Lower scores = more accessible.

| Audience type | FK Grade Level | FK Reading Ease | Notes |
|---------------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| General consumer | 6-8 | 60-70 | Mass market, DTC, broad B2C |
| Business professional | 8-10 | 50-60 | B2B, SaaS, SMB |
| Technical / specialist | 10-12 | 30-50 | DevTools, enterprise, medical |
| Executive | 8-9 | 50-60 | C-suite reads for skimmability, not depth |

**Rule of thumb:** Write one grade level below your estimate of your audience. Readers find it easier to read down than up. A VP can read grade 7 copy without feeling patronized; a junior marketer cannot read grade 14 copy without giving up.

---

## Sentence Length Guidelines

These are targets, not strict rules. The goal is variance, not a specific average.

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Average sentence length | 15 words |
| Minimum sentence length (in mix) | 5-6 words |
| Maximum sentence length (in mix) | 25-30 words |
| Variance | High: at least 2x difference between your shortest and longest sentences |

**What to avoid:**
- All sentences in the 14-18 word range (AI pattern; reads as monotone)
- Sentences over 35 words without a pause (subordinate clause or semicolon). Reader loses the thread.
- Three sentences under 7 words in a row without a longer one to anchor (choppy)

**The variance test:** If you read the first 10 sentences aloud and they all land at roughly the same beat, the rhythm is off. Vary deliberately.

## Paragraph Length Guidelines

| Copy type | Max lines per paragraph | Reason |
|-----------|------------------------|--------|
| Email (mobile) | 2-3 lines | Small screen; long blocks skip |
| Landing page | 3-4 lines | High scan rate; readers look for headers |
| Blog / editorial | 4-6 lines | Longer format allows more development |
| Social (LinkedIn) | 1-2 lines | Algorithm favors short paragraphs with line breaks |
| Sales page | 2-4 lines | Mix short (punchy) and medium (credibility) |

**Hard rule:** Never more than 5 lines in any paragraph in marketing copy. Academic conventions don't apply here.

## Grade Level by Copy Type

| Copy type | Target grade | Rationale |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Email subject lines | 4-6 | Scanned in 2 seconds; simple wins |
| Email body | 6-8 | Conversational register |
| Landing page hero | 6-7 | Immediate comprehension required |
| Landing page body | 8-9 | More room for specifics |
| Ad copy | 4-6 | Short-form; clarity over depth |
| Blog posts (general) | 8-9 | Slightly more depth than marketing |
| Thought leadership | 10-11 | Audience expects more sophisticated framing |
| Case studies | 9-10 | Data-heavy; some complexity is expected |
| UX copy (buttons/labels) | 3-5 | One action, one word ideally |
| UX copy (error messages) | 6-7 | Clarity and empathy; no jargon |

## Practical Readability Checks

These don't require a tool, just a read-through:

**Passive voice audit:** Identify every "is/was/are/were + past participle" construction. Rewrite in active voice unless passive is clearly better. More than 15% passive = problem.

**Jargon audit:** Underline every industry term, acronym, or internal phrase. Ask: does my reader use this word themselves? If not, replace or define on first use.

**First-word repetition:** Check whether three or more consecutive sentences start with the same word or the same part of speech. Break the pattern.

**Long sentence rescue:** For any sentence over 25 words, find the conjunction or comma at the midpoint and break it into two sentences. If the sentence still reads correctly as two, keep both. If it loses meaning, restore the original.
