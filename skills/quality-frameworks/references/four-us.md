# The 4Us: Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful

Evaluation framework primarily for headlines, subject lines, and hooks. A strong headline hits at least 3 of 4. Hitting all 4 is rare and worth protecting when you find it.

Reference also: `headline-formulas/references/proven-patterns.md` for structural templates.

---

## Urgent

**Definition:** The reader feels a reason to act or read now, not later.

**What scores high:**
- Time-bound language that's specific and believable ("Closes Friday", "Last 12 spots")
- Stakes that expire or diminish ("Before the algorithm changes again")
- News angle ("Just released", "New research shows")
- The implication that waiting costs something

**What scores low:**
- Fake urgency ("Act now!" with no reason why now)
- Evergreen urgency ("Always important", "Essential reading")
- Urgency that doesn't connect to the reader's actual situation

**Scoring guide:**
- 3: Creates genuine time pressure with a specific reason
- 2: Implies time-sensitivity without hard constraint
- 1: No urgency; reader can return to this whenever

**Examples:**
- "Registration closes Thursday" (score 3)
- "5 things to read before your next campaign launch" (score 2)
- "How to write better headlines" (score 1)

## Unique

**Definition:** The headline makes a claim or takes an angle the reader hasn't seen a hundred times.

**What scores high:**
- A counterintuitive claim ("The worst time to send a cold email is Monday morning")
- A specific angle that contradicts common wisdom
- A hook built around a specific situation, not a broad topic
- An original framing of a familiar problem

**What scores low:**
- "How to [common task]" with no distinctive angle
- "5 ways to [improve X]" where the topic has 500 identical articles
- Any headline that the #1 Google result already uses
- Generic benefit promises ("Get more done in less time")

**Scoring guide:**
- 3: Reader thinks "I haven't seen this angle before"
- 2: Familiar topic, fresh-enough framing
- 1: Derivative; the headline could belong to any article on this topic

**Examples:**
- "Why your best subject line is probably too long" (score 3)
- "The cold email framework that's working right now" (score 2)
- "Cold email tips for B2B marketers" (score 1)

---

## Ultra-specific

**Definition:** The headline contains a detail that makes the claim concrete and believable.

**What scores high:**
- Exact numbers ("11 subject lines", "23% more opens", "7 days")
- Named sources, products, or methods
- Specific audience call-outs ("For B2B SaaS teams with under 50 employees")
- Timeframes, dollar amounts, percentages

**What scores low:**
- "Some tips", "better results", "more effective"
- Numbers that are too round (100%, 10x) to be credible
- Qualifiers that reduce specificity ("often", "in many cases", "can help")
- Broad audience framing ("For everyone who...")

**Scoring guide:**
- 3: Contains a specific number, name, or verifiable detail
- 2: Some specificity, but not pinned to a concrete claim
- 1: Vague; could describe anything

**Examples:**
- "These 3 subject lines got a 52% open rate last month" (score 3)
- "Subject lines that get more opens" (score 2)
- "Better email marketing" (score 1)

---

## Useful

**Definition:** The reader understands the value they'll get from reading or clicking.

**What scores high:**
- Clear benefit statement (what the reader walks away with)
- Addresses a real problem the audience has
- The payoff is visible before the click
- "Job to be done" framing (what problem does this solve for me right now?)

**What scores low:**
- Clever without payoff ("The secret they don't want you to know")
- Benefit buried or absent ("An interesting take on email marketing")
- Promise that's too big to be real ("The only email guide you'll ever need")
- Payoff is for the writer, not the reader ("Announcing our new feature")

**Scoring guide:**
- 3: Payoff is clear, specific, and the reader genuinely wants it
- 2: Useful topic, unclear what exactly the reader gets
- 1: Reader can't tell what they'll gain from this

**Examples:**
- "How to cut your cold email reply time from 3 days to 4 hours" (score 3)
- "Email strategies for faster responses" (score 2)
- "Rethinking cold outreach" (score 1)

## Scoring and Thresholds

| Total score | Assessment | Action |
|-------------|------------|--------|
| 10-12 | Exceptional | Use it |
| 7-9 | Strong | Publish |
| 4-6 | Acceptable | Try to improve one dimension |
| 1-3 | Weak | Rewrite; identify which U is failing |

**Minimum standard:** 3 out of 4 dimensions must score 2 or higher.

**Quick test:** Can you answer "why should I read/click this right now?" If the answer requires creativity or inference, the headline is not doing its job.
