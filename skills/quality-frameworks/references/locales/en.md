# English (en-GB, en-US, international)

Load this with `../anti-slop.md` for any English copy. The stock-phrase lint lists for English live in `../anti-slop.md`; this file holds conventions.

## Which English

- **en-GB / international European shops:** British spelling (flavour, colour, savoury, organise, -ise), "VAT", "delivery", "postage". A shop selling across Europe in English writes for readers whose first language is often not English: short sentences, no idioms that only work in one country, no sports metaphors.
- **en-US:** American spelling (flavor, color, savory, -ize), "shipping", "sales tax".
- Pick one spelling system per site and keep it. Mixed spelling on one page is an error.

## Typography and formats

- **Money:** `€22.13` (no space, decimal point) in English, even on a shop whose other language writes `€ 22,13`. Thousands with a comma: `€1,250`. Never copy the decimal comma from a Dutch or German fact sheet into English copy.
- **Numbers:** decimal point (`15.5%`), `10 °C`, `720 ml`. Ranges with an unspaced en dash or "to": `5–10 °C`, `5 to 10 °C`.
- **Headings and buttons:** sentence case in UI and shop copy ("Add to cart", "How to serve it"). Title Case only where the brand's existing style uses it.
- **Dashes:** see `../anti-slop.md` §8.
- **Japanese and other loanwords:** follow the shop's existing romanisation (Shuzō or Shuzo, but the same everywhere on a page); italics are not needed for common terms such as sake, umami or junmai.

## Translationese: what an English reader notices

Copy written from Dutch or German facts often keeps their habits:
- **Decimal commas and `€ 22,13`** in English text.
- **Calqued compounds:** "the yuzu sake line" for the brand's range ("Tsuruume range"), "taste profile" piled on every page.
- **Over-formal register** carried over from u/Sie: English shop copy addresses the reader as "you" and stays plain.
- **Long noun chains** from German: "the optimal serving temperature recommendation" → "serve it at 10 °C".

## Search terms

Use the target query exactly as given in the brief. English-language queries from European buyers are often product names ("kaiun honjozo") rather than "buy X"; do not add "buy" to body copy to match a keyword.
