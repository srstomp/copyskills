# German (de-DE, de-AT, de-CH)

Load this with `../anti-slop.md` for any German copy. Write German from the fact sheet in German; never translate a draft written in another language.

## Register

- Choose **Sie** or **du** from the brief or the voice samples. Without either: **Sie** for a general adult audience, B2B, finance, and premium or older audiences; **du** for lifestyle brands that address a young audience. Many German shops now use du; follow the brand, not a habit.
- Keep one register across the page and the site, including imperatives: "Servieren Sie ihn gut gekühlt" (Sie) or "Servier ihn gut gekühlt" (du). A du-imperative in a text that is otherwise neutral or formal is an error. A neutral infinitive avoids the choice in short instructions: "Gut gekühlt servieren."
- "Sie/Ihr/Ihnen" are capitalised; "du/dein" lowercase in web copy.

## Typography and formats

- **Money:** `22,13 €` (number, non-breaking space, euro sign) is the DIN 5008 form and the most common in German shops; `€ 22,13` also occurs. Follow the shop's existing format; never `€22.13`.
- **Numbers:** decimal comma, thousands point (`1.250`), `11 % Vol.` or `11 % vol` for alcohol, `10 °C`, `360 ml`.
- **Dates:** `17. November 2026` or `17.11.2026`.
- **Quotes:** „so“: the opening mark is low (U+201E „), the closing mark is high (U+201C “). A straight " as the closing mark is the most common error in model-written German; check every quote. Guillemets »so« are also correct when the shop uses them.
- **Dashes:** the spaced en dash ( – ) is the German Gedankenstrich and is correct typography. Use it rarely: more than one per paragraph is the model habit. The em dash (—) is not German typography and is an error.
- **Nouns are capitalised;** headings follow normal German capitalisation, not English Title Case for adjectives and verbs ("Sake mit Flaschengärung", not "Sake Mit Flaschengärung").
- **Compounds:** one word or hyphenated, never apart: "Sakebrauerei" or "Sake-Brauerei", "Reissorte", "Flaschengärung". "Sake Brauerei" is an error.
- **No genitive apostrophe:** "Shichikens Sparkling Sake", not "Shichiken's".

## Translationese: what a German reader notices

- **Calques:** "Sinn machen" → "sinnvoll sein"/"Sinn ergeben"; "am Ende des Tages" → "letztlich"; "in 2026" → "2026" or "im Jahr 2026"; "einmal mehr" for "once again" is fine, but "eine Menge" in formal copy is not.
- **Anglicisms where German has a normal word:** "Sparkling Sake" is common in the trade and fine; "Food Pairing" is fine in lifestyle copy; but "performen", "tasten", "Highlight" as filler, and English adjectives ("smooth", "fresh") in German sentences read as translated.
- **Verb position:** the finite verb is second in a main clause and last in a subordinate clause. A sentence that keeps English order reads as translated; rewrite from the meaning.
- **Nominal style:** German model copy piles up nouns ("die Sicherstellung der optimalen Trinktemperatur"). Use verbs: "damit er die richtige Temperatur hat".
- **Overlong sentences** with three subordinate clauses are not "native"; German shop copy is short and concrete.

## Stock phrasing in German copy

Watch for: "Tauchen Sie ein in", "Entdecken Sie", "Erleben Sie", "Genießen Sie" opening every paragraph; "ein wahres Geschmackserlebnis", "Gaumenfreude", "ein Muss für jeden", "perfekt für jeden Anlass", "die perfekte Wahl", "lassen Sie sich verzaubern", "auf das nächste Level", "integraler Bestandteil", "vielfältige Möglichkeiten", "Es ist wichtig zu beachten, dass", "In der heutigen (schnelllebigen) Zeit", "nicht nur ..., sondern auch", "Fazit:" or "Zusammenfassend" as a closer.

## Search terms

Use the target query from the brief exactly as German searchers type it ("Sake kaufen", "Shichiken Sparkling kaufen"). Never translate an English or Dutch keyword. In the title it may stand as is; in body copy, once, where it reads naturally.
