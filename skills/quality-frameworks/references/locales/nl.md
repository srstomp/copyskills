# Dutch (nl-NL, nl-BE)

Load this with `../anti-slop.md` for any Dutch copy. It covers what a native Dutch editor fixes in model-written or translated copy. Write Dutch from the fact sheet in Dutch; never translate a draft written in another language, because the sentence order and idiom of the source language survive the translation.

## Register

- **je/jij/jouw** is the default for consumer shops, apps and newsletters in the Netherlands. Use **u/uw** for B2B, finance, legal, healthcare, or an older audience, and when the brief or the voice samples use it.
- Flemish (nl-BE) consumer copy also uses je more and more, but u is safer for official or older audiences. Follow the voice samples.
- Pick one and keep it across the page and the whole site. Mixing je and u in one text is an error. Capitalised "U" is dated; write "u".
- Imperatives are normal and direct in Dutch shop copy ("Schenk hem koud in"), without "alsjeblieft".

## Typography and formats

- **Headings, titles, buttons:** sentence case. "Sake kopen bij ons", not "Sake Kopen Bij Ons". Only the first word and proper names get a capital.
- **Money:** `€ 22,13` (euro sign, space, decimal comma). Thousands with a point: `€ 1.250`. Round amounts: `€ 75` or `€ 75,-`; follow the shop's own style.
- **Numbers:** decimal comma (`15,5%`, `7,5 cl`); `%` directly after the number. Temperatures `10 °C`. Ranges `5-10 °C` or `5 tot 10 °C`.
- **Dates and times:** `17 november 2026`, months and weekdays lowercase; `17.00 uur`.
- **Dashes:** as in `../anti-slop.md` §8: no em dash or spaced dash as a connector. Dutch uses a colon or a comma where English copy uses a dash.
- **Compounds are written as one word.** "yuzusake", "sakefles", "proefnotities", "verzendkosten", "garnalentempura". Writing them apart ("yuzu sake", "sake fles") is the "Engelse ziekte" and an error. Use a hyphen only for a vowel clash ("sake-expert") or with a proper name or abbreviation ("Shizuoka-gisten", "IWC-titel").
- **Place names:** drop English administrative labels: "Kainan" not "Kainan City", "de prefectuur Shizuoka" or just "Shizuoka".
- **Quotes:** ‘enkele’ or “dubbele” aanhalingstekens, curly in finished copy unless the shop's own pages use straight ones; one style per site.

## Translationese: what a Dutch reader notices

These come from writing English in Dutch words. Check every sentence.

- **No comma between subject and verb.** English-style list subjects lose their agreement: "Meloen, witte bloesem en gestoomde rijst, geeft..." must be "Meloen, witte bloesem en gestoomde rijst geven...". Check every verb after a list.
- **A comma between two finite verbs** is Dutch: "Wie dit proeft, wil meer." English comma habits elsewhere (before "en" in a short list, after a short opening phrase) are not.
- **Possessives:** English puts "your" everywhere; Dutch uses the article. "Schenk hem in een klein glas", not "in jouw kleine glas".
- **Deixis:** on a Dutch shop "hier" means the Netherlands. "De familie brouwt daar sinds 1872", not "hier".
- **Calques to rewrite:** "maakt (geen) zin" → "is (niet) logisch"; "aan het eind van de dag" → "uiteindelijk"; "in termen van" → "wat betreft"; "wees de eerste die" → rewrite; "het is aan jou" → "jij kiest"; "de [X]-lijn" for a product line → "de [X]-serie" or "het merk [X]"; "middenmoot" for the middle of a taste (it means an average ranking) → "het midden", "in de mond".
- **Wrong intensity:** "ijskoud" is ice-cold, not fridge-cold; "koelkastkoud" or "goed gekoeld". "Kurkdroog" is idiomatic and fine. Check that a translated adjective keeps the strength of the fact.
- **Word order:** Dutch puts the finite verb second and the rest of the verb cluster at the end. A sentence that keeps English order reads as translated even when it is grammatical; rewrite it from the meaning.
- **Passive pile-ups** ("wordt gemaakt door ... en wordt geserveerd") read as translated; use an active verb.

## Stock phrasing in Dutch copy

Dutch model copy has its own set of defaults. The lint lists below hold the ones that are almost never right in shop copy. Also watch for: "een ware", "pure verwennerij", "smaaksensatie", "smaakexplosie", "laat je verrassen door", "stap in de wereld van", "een reis door", "voor de echte liefhebber", "tijdloos", "authentiek", "uniek", "ambachtelijk" (fine when the fact sheet says how it is made by hand; a tell as decoration), and adjective triplets before a noun ("een strakke, levendige en kurkdroge afdronk").

## Search terms

- Use the target query from the brief exactly as Dutch searchers type it ("umeshu kopen", "sake kopen"). Never translate an English keyword; Dutch search behaviour differs per term and needs research or a brief.
- In a title tag the query can stand as is ("Umeshu kopen | Shopnaam"). In body copy, use it once where it reads naturally; "Zoek je een umeshu om te kopen" is keyword stuffing.
