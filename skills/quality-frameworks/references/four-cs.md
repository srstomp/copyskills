# The 4Cs: Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible

A quality framework for evaluating copy at the sentence and section level. Use for any copy type. Works alongside the scoring rubric: the 4Cs map most directly to Clarity (Clear, Concise) and Persuasion (Compelling, Credible).

---

## Clear

**Definition:** The reader understands what you mean on first read. No re-reading, no guessing.

**What scores high:**
- One idea per sentence
- Subject and verb close together at the start of the sentence
- Specific nouns (not "solution" or "platform" but the actual product name or capability)
- Technical terms only when the audience uses them
- Active voice in most sentences

**What scores low:**
- Buried subject: "In order to achieve the kind of results that our enterprise customers have come to expect from a solution of this type..."
- Nominalization: turning verbs into nouns ("provide assistance" instead of "help", "make a decision" instead of "decide")
- Hedging: "may potentially be able to", "in many cases", "quite possibly"
- Ambiguous pronouns: "it", "this", "they" with no clear referent

**Before:**
> "Leveraging our platform's capabilities, teams can realize efficiency gains across their entire workflow ecosystem, enabling them to focus on higher-value activities."

**After:**
> "Teams using Relay cut the time they spend on manual coordination by 40%. That time goes back to actual work."

---

## Concise

**Definition:** Every word earns its place. No filler, no padding, no repetition.

**What scores high:**
- Sentences that can't be shortened without losing meaning
- Zero throat-clearing phrases ("It's worth noting that...", "As I mentioned earlier...")
- Tight CTAs ("Start free trial" beats "Click here to get started with your free trial today")
- Active voice (shorter than passive by default)

**What scores low:**
- Redundant pairs: "each and every", "first and foremost", "true and accurate"
- Pleonasms: "completely finished", "future plans", "past history"
- Padding to hit word count: "In conclusion, what we've seen is that..."
- Restatement without addition: stating the same point twice with different words

**Before:**
> "Our team of dedicated professionals is fully committed to providing you with the highest level of customer service and support at all times, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week."

**After:**
> "Support is available 24/7."

---

## Compelling

**Definition:** The copy creates a pull. The reader wants to keep reading or take action.

**What scores high:**
- Opens with the reader's problem, not the company's product
- Stakes are clear: what changes if the reader acts vs. doesn't act
- One strong specific detail beats three vague claims
- Voice has personality: a point of view is visible
- The reader can see themselves in the scenario described

**What scores low:**
- Feature dumps without benefit translation
- Passive observer framing: "Companies today are increasingly..."
- Neutral, hedged language that avoids commitment
- Copy that could apply to any reader (too broad to land for anyone)
- No tension or stakes: the reader feels nothing

**Before:**
> "Relay offers a range of features designed to help marketing teams work more effectively, including collaboration tools, reporting dashboards, and integrations with popular platforms."

**After:**
> "Your team spends 6 hours a week syncing in Slack about campaigns that already launched. Relay kills that loop. One view, everyone aligned, no update meetings."

---

## Credible

**Definition:** The reader believes you. Claims are substantiated or are inherently believable.

**What scores high:**
- Specific numbers (exact beats approximate)
- Named customers with visible attribution
- Mechanism explanation: not just "it works" but a brief "here's why it works"
- Appropriate hedging: "in our experience" is more credible than a universal claim
- Third-party validation (press, research, awards that the audience knows)

**What scores low:**
- Superlatives without evidence: "the best", "the only", "unmatched"
- Vague social proof: "thousands of customers love us"
- Claims that violate common sense: "10x your results in 30 days"
- Inconsistency between copy and reader experience (promising easy when product has a learning curve)
- Anonymous testimonials

**Before:**
> "Trusted by thousands of the world's leading companies, our industry-leading solution delivers unmatched results that consistently exceed expectations."

**After:**
> "Used by 2,400 marketing teams, including the teams at Notion, Linear, and Vercel. Median customer sees a 28% lift in campaign throughput in the first 90 days."

---

## Applying the 4Cs Together

These are not a checklist. They interact:

- **Clear but not compelling:** The reader understands but doesn't care. Add stakes, specifics, voice.
- **Compelling but not credible:** Exciting claims the reader doesn't believe. Add proof, numbers, attribution.
- **Concise but not clear:** Short enough that meaning is lost. Find where you cut too much and restore the essential context.
- **Credible but not concise:** Wall of evidence. No one reads it. Lead with the single strongest proof point.

Priority order when there's a tension: **Clear > Credible > Compelling > Concise.** Never sacrifice clarity for brevity. Never sacrifice credibility for punch.
