# Pricing Pages

Pricing page copy is the highest-stakes copy on most SaaS sites. The reader is already interested. Bad copy at this stage loses a buyer who was ready to convert.

The four most common pricing page copy failures: bad tier names, feature-first descriptions, missing objection handling, and wrong visual anchoring.

---

## Tier Naming

### What not to name your plans

**"Basic"** is the single worst plan name in SaaS. Nobody wants to be basic. "Basic" signals that this is the lesser, stripped-down, not-quite-good-enough option. Buyers who are price-sensitive still want to feel like they made a smart choice, not a compromised one.

**"Standard"** and **"Regular"** have the same problem. They communicate average. The reader's internal response is "if I can afford it, I should not be on the standard plan."

**"Enterprise" as your only high tier** signals "call us and we'll tell you when we stop laughing." Use Enterprise for custom arrangements only, and pair it with real pricing or a "contact us" prompt that feels like a hand-off, not a wall.

### Tier naming principles

Names should communicate aspiration and trajectory, not feature count.

| Tier position | Naming principle | Examples |
|---------------|------------------|---------|
| Entry tier | Where you start, not where you're stuck | Starter, Free, Solo, Launch, Essentials |
| Mid tier | Where you grow, the obvious choice | Growth, Pro, Team, Build, Plus |
| Top tier | Where you scale, signals ambition | Scale, Business, Enterprise, Advanced, Max |

**The test for a tier name:** Would the buyer at that tier feel like the name describes where they want to be? If not, rename it.

A solo freelancer on "Starter" does not feel diminished -- they are starting. A 12-person team on "Growth" feels appropriate -- they are growing. A 200-person company on "Scale" feels right -- they are scaling. None of these readers feel like they settled.

## Plan Structure and the Decoy Effect

### Anchoring

Show the most expensive tier first (left to right on desktop, or top to bottom on mobile). When readers see the highest price first, the middle tier registers as a savings, not a cost. When they see the cheapest tier first, the middle tier feels like an upgrade expense.

**Wrong order (cheapest first):**
Starter ($29) | Growth ($79) | Scale ($149)
Reader's frame: "The $79 plan costs a lot more than the $29 plan."

**Right order (most expensive first):**
Scale ($149) | Growth ($79) | Starter ($29)
Reader's frame: "The $79 plan is almost half the price of the top tier."

### The Decoy Principle

The middle tier should be the obvious choice. Structure it so:
- It includes significantly more value than the entry tier (not just one extra feature)
- It stops short of the complexity or cost of the top tier
- Its price-per-feature ratio is visibly better than either adjacent tier

The middle tier is your decoy. Its job is to make itself look so obviously right that buyers self-select into it without needing to be sold.

**Decoy tier checklist:**
- [ ] Price gap between entry and mid feels justified by the feature jump
- [ ] Mid tier includes the one feature that most buyers actually want (seats, storage, integrations, analytics)
- [ ] Mid tier is "highlighted" visually (badge, color, "Most Popular" label) to draw attention
- [ ] Top tier's main differentiator is something mid-tier buyers don't need yet (SSO, custom contracts, dedicated support)

## Feature Descriptions: Benefit-First, Not Feature-First

Most pricing pages list features. Buyers read past feature lists because they cannot translate specs into value on their own. Do the translation for them.

**Feature-first (what most pricing pages look like):**
- Unlimited projects
- API access
- Priority support
- Advanced analytics

**Benefit-first (what converts):**
- Work across all your clients without hitting limits
- Connect to your existing tools in an afternoon, no engineering required
- Get answers in under 4 hours, not 2 business days
- See which campaigns are driving revenue, not just traffic

**The translation rule:** For each feature, ask "why does this matter to the buyer at this tier?" Write that answer, not the feature name.

Some features are self-evident to a technical audience (SSO, SAML, audit logs). For those readers, the feature name is enough. For everything else, do the translation work.

## FAQ Copy Patterns

Pricing page FAQs should preempt the questions that stop people from buying, not answer the questions that do not matter.

### Questions that preempt purchase blockers

**"Can I change plans later?"**
Yes. Upgrade or downgrade at any time. If you upgrade mid-cycle, we prorate the difference. If you downgrade, the change takes effect at your next renewal.

**"What happens when my trial ends?"**
You'll get an email 3 days before your trial ends. If you don't add a payment method, your account moves to the free plan. Your data stays intact.

**"Is there a contract?"**
Monthly plans have no contract. Annual plans are paid upfront and save 20%. Both cancel with one click.

**"Do I need a credit card to start?"**
Not for the free trial. You add payment details only when you choose to upgrade.

### Questions that signal hesitation

If buyers are asking these questions before purchasing, address them on the page before they reach the FAQ:

- "Does this work for [specific use case]?" (signal: the page copy is too generic)
- "How is this different from [competitor]?" (signal: the value proposition is weak)
- "Is my data secure?" (signal: trust signals are missing from the page)

If these questions appear in your support tickets or on-page behavior (via heat maps or chat), add answers to the FAQ AND fix the underlying copy gap they reveal.

## Money-Back Guarantee Copy

Guarantees reduce purchase anxiety at the moment of highest friction. The copy must be specific to be credible.

**Weak guarantee (does not reduce anxiety):**
"We offer a 30-day money-back guarantee."

**Strong guarantee (reduces anxiety and builds trust):**
"Not the right fit? Tell us within 30 days for a full refund, no questions asked. We'll process it within 2 business days."

The difference: specific timeline for the refund, no conditional language ("no questions asked"), and human language ("tell us") instead of bureaucratic language ("contact our refund department").

Place the guarantee:
- Adjacent to the primary CTA on the pricing page
- As a trust signal below the CTA button (not in a separate section nobody reads)
- In the checkout confirmation email (reinforces the decision the buyer just made)

## Objection Handling on Pricing Pages

Place objection-handling copy next to the friction that triggers the objection.

| Objection | Where it appears | Copy response pattern |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| "It's too expensive" | Adjacent to price | ROI framing or cost-of-not-acting |
| "I'm not sure I need this" | Below feature list | Specific use case or outcome example |
| "What if it doesn't work for us?" | Near CTA | Trial or guarantee removes the risk |
| "Is it worth switching from [X]?" | Comparison section | Specific differentiator + migration help |
| "We're too small / too big" | Near tier descriptions | Tier targeting copy (who each tier is for) |

The goal is not to handle every objection. It is to handle the one or two objections that are actually stopping your specific buyers. Check support tickets, sales call notes, or lost deal reasons to identify which objections matter for your product.

## Before/After: Pricing Page Tier Copy

**Before (feature-first, weak tier names):**

**Basic - $29/month**
- 5 projects
- 2 users
- Email support
- Basic reporting

**Pro - $79/month**
- Unlimited projects
- 10 users
- Priority support
- Advanced reporting
- API access

**Enterprise - $149/month**
- Everything in Pro
- Unlimited users
- Dedicated account manager
- Custom integrations
- SSO

---

**After (benefit-first, aspirational tier names, anchored correctly):**

**Scale - $149/month** (show this first)
For teams managing complex programs across multiple stakeholders.
- Your whole organization in one workspace, no seat caps
- A dedicated point of contact who knows your account (not a ticket queue)
- SSO and SAML for your IT team's peace of mind
- Custom integrations built to your spec

**Growth - $79/month** (highlighted as "Most Popular")
For teams that are past the scrappy phase and need room to build.
- Every client, every project, no limits
- Ten team members building together
- Priority support with a 4-hour response window
- The analytics that tell you what's actually working

**Starter - $29/month**
For solo operators and small teams getting their first clients.
- Five active projects running at once
- Two seats to bring in a collaborator
- Email support with next-business-day replies
- The core reports you need to stay on track
