# Brief Questions

Question bank for copy-brief Mode 1 (interview). Questions are organized by domain and field. Select the question that fits the copy type. Ask one question at a time.

---

## Universal Questions

These apply to every copy type. Ask in this order.

**type** (required)
> What are you writing? (e.g., cold email, landing page, product description, onboarding message)

**goal** (required)
> What should the reader do after reading this? Be specific: book a call, sign up, click through, reply.

**audience.who** (required)
> Who is this for? Describe them: role, age range, context, or however you think about them.

**audience.pain**
> What's the problem they're trying to solve, or the frustration they're sitting with right now?

**audience.sophistication**
> How much do they already know about this topic? Are they a beginner, familiar, or expert?

**product.name**
> What's the product or service called?

**product.description**
> What does it actually do? One or two sentences, in plain terms.

**product.differentiator**
> What makes it different from the alternatives they're already aware of?

**brand_voice.tone**
> How should the writing sound? Pick a few adjectives: direct, warm, technical, playful, confident, etc.

**brand_voice.avoids**
> Any words or phrases to avoid? Things that feel off-brand or that you're tired of seeing?

**brand_voice.examples**
> Can you share an example of writing that sounds right? Even a sentence or two from an email or ad you liked.

**constraints.length**
> Any length limit? Word count, character count, or just a sense of short vs. long.

**constraints.cta**
> What's the call to action? One link, a reply, a form, or something else?

## Email-Specific Questions

Use when `type` includes "email", "drip", "sequence", "newsletter", or "outreach".

**Sequence context**
> Is this a single email or part of a sequence? If it's part of a sequence, what came before it?

**Trigger**
> What triggers this email? A sign-up, a purchase, inactivity, a date, a behavior?

**Relationship**
> What's the relationship with the recipient? Cold (never heard of you), warm (some prior contact), or existing customer?

**Subject line**
> Do you need a subject line too, or just the body?

**Tone constraint**
> Any specific tone this email needs to match? (e.g., to match previous emails in the sequence)

---

## Marketing Copy Questions

Use when `type` includes "landing page", "ad", "headline", "tagline", "CTA button", or "campaign".

**Placement**
> What page or format is this for? Hero section, pricing page, Google ad, Facebook ad?

**Objections**
> What objections does your audience have? What stops them from buying or signing up?

**Social proof**
> Do you have proof points to include: testimonials, customer counts, notable clients, stats?

**Competing alternatives**
> What are they currently using instead? What does switching away from that require?

**Campaign context**
> Is this part of a larger campaign? Any theme, message, or visual context to stay consistent with?

---

## UX Copy Questions

Use when `type` includes "onboarding", "tooltip", "error message", "button", "empty state", "confirmation", or "UI copy".

**Screen and component**
> What screen or component is this for? Where does the user see it?

**Action**
> What action should the user take, or what just happened that triggered this message?

**Emotional state**
> What's the user's emotional state at this point in the flow? Confused, excited, hesitant, relieved?

**Technical context**
> Is there a technical constraint on length? Character limits, truncation, or display size?

**Error source**
> If this is an error message: what caused the error, and what can the user do to fix it?

---

## Follow-Up Probes

Use these when a recommended field is missing and the user's first answer is vague.

**For audience.who (vague answer)**
> You said [their answer]. What's a specific person who fits that? Job title, situation, what they were doing before they found you?

**For audience.pain (vague answer)**
> What does that frustration actually feel like for them day to day? What's the moment it gets annoying?

**For brand_voice (no examples given)**
> Think of a brand whose writing you respect, even outside your industry. What is it about their tone that works?

**For goal (vague answer, e.g., "awareness")**
> What does success look like for this specific piece? If it works, what happens next for the reader?

**For product.differentiator (vague answer)**
> If a prospect said "I can already get that from [competitor]", what would you say?
