# Cold Outreach Reference

Structures, patterns, and examples for cold outreach and follow-up sequences.

## The Core Constraint

Cold outreach has no goodwill budget. The recipient did not ask for this email. You have roughly one sentence to give them a reason to keep reading.

That sentence cannot be about you.

---

## First-Touch Structure

Four components. Keep total length under 150 words. Plain text only.

### 1. Personalization hook (1 sentence)
A specific observation about the recipient, their company, their recent work, or something they said publicly. Not a compliment. An observation.

- "Saw the piece you published on why enterprise sales cycles keep getting longer -- you're the second founder I've heard frame it that way."
- "Noticed you just opened a Denver office. Guessing hiring is the thing keeping you up right now."
- "Your rebrand landed well. The positioning shifted pretty meaningfully from last year."

The hook must be specific enough that it could not have been sent to 1,000 people. If you could swap in another company name and the sentence still works, it is not specific enough.

### 2. Pain or insight (1-2 sentences)
Name a problem that someone in their role, at a company their size, at their stage, typically has. Or share a finding that reframes how they think about a problem they already know they have.

Do not introduce your product yet.

### 3. Bridge to offer (1 sentence)
One sentence connecting the problem or insight to what you do. No buzzwords. No category language ("AI-powered", "end-to-end", "best-in-class"). Tell them what you actually do.

### 4. Soft CTA (1 sentence)
Ask for a small commitment. Not a 45-minute call. Not a demo. Something that costs them nothing.

- "Would it be useful if I sent over how [Company X] handled this?"
- "Worth a quick call this week to see if it's relevant?"
- "Happy to share what we've seen work at similar companies if you want to take a look."

Do not ask for the sale in a cold email.

## What Not to Do

**Long intros**: "My name is [Name] and I work at [Company] and we help companies like yours..." -- Delete everything before the first piece of value.

**Generic openings**: "I hope you're doing well." / "Congrats on the recent funding!" (without anything specific to say about it) / "I came across your profile and was impressed." -- These signal template. Delete.

**Multiple CTAs**: "We could set up a demo, or if you'd prefer I can send a case study, or maybe a quick intro call?" -- Pick one.

**Attachment-heavy emails**: Do not attach a PDF, deck, or one-pager to a cold email. They kill deliverability and signal spam.

**The feature dump**: listing what your product does instead of naming the problem it solves.

**The passive ask**: "Let me know if you're ever interested." -- This is not a CTA. It gives the recipient no reason to respond now.

## Follow-Up Sequence (3-touch)

### Touch 2: Value-add (3-5 days after touch 1)
Do not follow up with "just checking in." Send something useful.

- A relevant article or data point
- A brief case study (2-3 sentences, not an attachment)
- A short insight about their industry or a shift you've noticed

The implicit message: this person sends things worth reading.

Length: 60-80 words.

### Touch 3: Social proof (5-7 days after touch 2)
Name a customer they would recognize (with permission), or describe a situation similar to theirs with a specific outcome.

"We just wrapped a project with [Company] -- they were dealing with exactly the [problem]. Happy to share how it turned out."

Length: 60-80 words.

### Touch 4: Breakup (5-7 days after touch 3)
Short. Direct. Give them an easy out.

"I'll stop reaching out after this one. If the timing is ever right, I'm easy to find. And if I've missed the mark on what you're dealing with, let me know -- I'd rather know than guess."

This email often gets responses. The lack of pressure is the hook.

## Personalization Patterns

### Company-specific
- Reference a recent product launch, press mention, or hiring trend
- Comment on something they published (blog post, job description, investor update)
- Note a competitive move in their market that affects them

### Role-specific
- Name the constraint that is typical for someone in their exact role
- Reference a shift in how their role is evolving (e.g., "VP Sales increasingly being pulled into product decisions")

### Trigger-event-based
- New funding round: assume they are now under pressure to grow faster
- Leadership hire: often signals a strategy shift or a problem with the previous approach
- New office or market expansion: signals scaling challenges
- Product launch: signals they are investing in a new bet

Trigger events are the highest-signal personalization hooks. Act on them within 1-2 weeks of the event. Stale trigger events read as lazy research.

## Example Cold Emails

These are complete, send-ready examples. They are written for a B2B SaaS company that helps mid-market companies reduce time-to-hire for engineering roles.

### Example 1: Trigger-event-based (new funding)

**Subject:** your Series B and engineering hiring

You just closed a $22M round -- congrats. Guessing the next 12 months are going to be heavy on engineering headcount.

Most companies at your stage find that their recruiting process, which was fine at 20 engineers, starts grinding at 50. Screening volume spikes, the hiring bar gets inconsistent, and senior engineers start spending 4-5 hours a week in interviews instead of building.

We help teams get from "open req" to "offer signed" faster without pulling eng out of product work. Worth seeing if it's relevant to what you're building?

[Name]

---

### Example 2: Role-specific (VP Engineering)

**Subject:** engineering hiring at your scale

Saw that Acme added 14 engineering roles in Q1. That kind of ramp usually means interview load is becoming a real drag on the team.

We work with VP Engs at companies your size who are trying to keep hiring quality high without burning out the people doing the hiring. Most are spending 6-8 hours per week per open role on screening alone.

Happy to share what we've seen work at similar companies if that's worth 20 minutes.

[Name]

---

### Example 3: Content-hook (something they published)

**Subject:** your note on hiring bar consistency

Your post on maintaining hiring bar as you scale struck a nerve with a few of our customers -- specifically the part about different interviewers weighting different things.

That problem tends to get worse as you add more interviewers. Calibration breaks down, and you end up with inconsistent offers and re-opens.

We've built tooling specifically around that piece of the process. Would it be useful if I sent over how a similar-sized team solved it?

[Name]

## Subject Line Guidance for Cold Outreach

- Target: 30-40 characters
- Lowercase performs as well as title case for cold email
- Avoid words that trigger spam filters: "free", "guaranteed", "limited time", "exclusive offer"
- Best performing patterns: topic + context ("engineering hiring at your scale"), direct question ("worth a conversation?"), shared reference ("your post on X")
- Do not write clickbait subject lines. If the email does not deliver on the subject line, you get reported.
