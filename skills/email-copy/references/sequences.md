# Email Sequences Reference

Structures, arc principles, and cadence guidelines for every major sequence type.

## Sequence Arc Principle

Every email in a sequence must satisfy two constraints simultaneously:

1. **Standalone legibility**: a subscriber who missed the previous email should be able to read this one and understand what is happening and what to do.
2. **Series momentum**: each email should acknowledge where the subscriber is in the journey and move them one step forward.

Do not write email 3 assuming email 2 was read. Do not write email 3 as if it has no relationship to email 2. Both are true at once.

## Welcome Sequence (3-5 emails)

**Goal**: convert a new subscriber into an engaged user who understands your value.

**Framework**: BAB (Before-After-Bridge). The sequence as a whole moves from "where you were before signing up" to "where you can be" and bridges the gap with the product.

### Email 1: Introduction and expectation-setting
- Send: Day 0 (immediately after signup)
- Goal: confirm the right address, set expectations for what comes next
- Structure: warm acknowledgment of their signup + one sentence on what they'll get + single low-friction CTA (e.g., "here's your free guide" or "confirm your email")
- Length: short. 80-120 words.
- Do not oversell. They just signed up. They know what they signed up for.

### Email 2: Core value demonstration
- Send: Day 1
- Goal: show the most important thing your product or content does
- Structure: name one specific problem your subscribers typically have + show how you address it + CTA to try or read the thing that demonstrates it
- Length: 120-180 words.

### Email 3: Feature or content highlight
- Send: Day 3
- Goal: surface a feature or resource they may not have discovered yet
- Structure: "One thing people often miss when they first join..." + the thing + why it matters + CTA to try it
- Length: 100-150 words.

### Email 4: Social proof
- Send: Day 7
- Goal: reduce doubt with a real story
- Structure: one customer story or testimonial (name, what they were trying to do, what changed) + CTA tied to that outcome
- Do not use vague testimonials ("It's amazing!"). Use specifics ("We cut our onboarding time from 3 weeks to 4 days").
- Length: 120-200 words.

### Email 5: Activation CTA (optional, for product sequences)
- Send: Day 10
- Goal: push toward the one action that turns a subscriber into an active user
- Structure: direct. What is the one action that matters most? Name it. Explain why it matters. Make it easy.
- Length: 80-120 words. This is not the place for a long read.

## Onboarding Sequence (5-7 emails)

**Goal**: move a new user from "just signed up" to "using the core loop regularly."

**Framework**: BAB. Each email bridges from the user's current state to a more capable state.

### Email 1: Quick win (Day 0)
- Get them to a tangible result in under 5 minutes
- Name the exact result they can achieve, not a vague "get started"

### Email 2: Core feature (Day 1)
- The single feature that, if they use it, makes them 10x more likely to stick
- One feature only. Do not introduce three features in one email.

### Email 3: Second feature (Day 3)
- A feature that compounds the value of the first
- Frame it as "now that you've done X, here's what makes X even better"

### Email 4: Integration or workflow (Day 5)
- How this product fits into their existing workflow or connects to tools they already use
- Use a concrete scenario: "If you're using [common tool], here's how this connects"

### Email 5: Advanced feature (Day 7)
- Something that rewards users who are already engaged
- Gate this behind "if you've already tried X" so beginners don't feel lost

### Email 6: Check-in (Day 10)
- Ask how it's going. Genuinely.
- Short email. Ask one question. Give them a way to reply.
- This email drives support conversations and surfaces objections early.

### Email 7: Upgrade or commitment nudge (Day 14)
- For freemium or trial products: this is the conversion email
- Do not lead with price. Lead with what they can do that they currently cannot.
- One clear CTA. No alternatives.

## Nurture Sequence (4-6 emails)

**Goal**: keep a warm list engaged and moving toward a purchase decision over weeks or months.

**Framework**: PAS. The sequence identifies a problem the subscriber has, agitates the cost of not solving it, and positions the solution.

### Email 1: Insight (Week 1)
- Open with a counterintuitive observation or data point about the subscriber's world
- Not a product email. A thinking email. Show you understand their industry.

### Email 2: Problem framing (Week 2)
- Name the specific problem in the subscriber's terms
- Agitate: what does it cost them when this problem is not solved?
- Light mention of the solution category (not your product specifically)

### Email 3: Solution framing (Week 3)
- Introduce your approach to the problem
- Not "our product has X feature." Frame it as "the way we think about this problem is..."
- One CTA to learn more or see a case study.

### Email 4: Proof (Week 4)
- A specific customer story tied to the problem you named in email 2
- Before/after with numbers if possible. Name the customer if allowed.

### Email 5: Offer (Week 5)
- The ask. Clear, specific, with a reason to act now.
- One CTA.

### Email 6: Re-ask or objection handler (Week 6, optional)
- For subscribers who did not act on email 5
- Acknowledge the hesitation ("Maybe you're wondering about X")
- Address the most common objection directly
- Repeat the CTA

## Re-engagement Sequence (3 emails)

**Goal**: win back subscribers who have gone quiet (no opens in 60-90 days).

**Framework**: PAS or BAB. Choose based on tone of the brand.

Send this sequence to a segment defined by inactivity. Do not send it to your whole list.

### Email 1: "We miss you" (Day 30 of inactivity)
- Acknowledge the silence without guilt-tripping
- Lead with what has changed or improved since they last engaged
- Soft CTA: "here's what you've missed" or "take another look"
- Subject line should signal something is different -- not just "we miss you"

### Email 2: Value re-anchor (Day 37)
- The best content or case study you have produced recently
- No re-engagement framing. Just send good stuff.
- The implicit message is: this is what you've been missing.

### Email 3: Last chance (Day 44)
- Be direct: "We're going to remove you from the list unless you want to stay."
- Give them a clear one-click way to stay
- This email also serves as a list hygiene tool. Non-responders should be removed.
- Subject line: direct and honest. "Should we keep sending you emails?"

## Cadence Guidelines

| Sequence type | Day 0 | Day 1 | Day 3 | Day 7 | Day 10 | Day 14 |
|--------------|-------|-------|-------|-------|--------|--------|
| Welcome | Email 1 | Email 2 | Email 3 | Email 4 | Email 5 | -- |
| Onboarding | Email 1 | Email 2 | Email 3 | Email 5 | Email 6 | Email 7 |
| Re-engagement | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |

For nurture sequences, weekly cadence is standard. Biweekly is acceptable for high-content industries (B2B, research-heavy). Do not send nurture sequences more than once per week.

For re-engagement: days 30, 37, and 44 from last activity. Not from list signup.

**Do not accelerate cadence to hit a launch deadline.** If a sequence is designed for 14 days and the launch is in 7 days, cut emails, do not compress timing.
